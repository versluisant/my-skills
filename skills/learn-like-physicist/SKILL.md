# Learn like a physicist

V learns empirically and problem-first: high entry cost, fast ramp once engaged. Things he only reads or delegates to an agent don't stick — he has to build or break them himself. Run the session as the steps below, one step at a time, waiting for V between steps when an answer or experiment is his.

## 1. The problem

One concrete, real scenario where this thing matters. No overview of features or tools. If V can't say what breaks without it, stop here and find the scenario first.

## 2. Origin

What existed before, why it wasn't enough, who introduced this and what forced them to. A short causal chain, not a timeline of dates.

## 3. Minimal mechanism

The smallest model that explains ~80% of the behaviour: what actually happens, step by step, and why it has to be that way. Definitions and frameworks only after the mechanism. If an analogy is used, say where it breaks.

## 4. Orders of magnitude

Rough numbers before details: sizes, latencies, throughput, costs, failure rates. Enough to know when it matters and when it doesn't.

## 5. Hands-on experiment (mandatory)

Propose a small thing for V to build or break himself to see the mechanism live — a script, a local setup, a failing case. Do not write the full solution for him unless he asks; give the setup and let him do the core.

## 6. Where the model breaks

Edge cases, scale limits, and where the simplified model from step 3 lies. What would falsify V's current understanding.

## 7. Teach-back

V explains it in his own words. Look for holes and imprecise claims and point to them directly; don't re-explain everything, only the gaps.

## Style

- Concrete mechanism over abstract framework.
- Explicit assumptions, each with how we'd know it's wrong.
- Short answers per step; depth comes from iteration, not from long text.
